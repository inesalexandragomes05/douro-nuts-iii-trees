The Douro crosses the region westward from Spain, fed from the north by the Sabor, Tua and Corgo and from the south by the Côa, Távora and Varosa. Rainfall follows a steep west–east gradient: over 1,400 mm on the Marão barrier down to 400–600 mm in the Douro Superior — the driest point of the territory at Barca d'Alva. Three large Douro dams regulate the main stem inside the NUTS III (Pocinho, Valeira, Régua) plus Foz Tua on the Tua; hill terraces and small weirs convey the rest to vineyard and olive plots.
